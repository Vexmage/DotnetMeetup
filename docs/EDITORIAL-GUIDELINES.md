# EugDotNet — Editorial Guidelines and Publishing Standards

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the editorial mission, intellectual standards, journalistic principles, publishing conventions, and contributor expectations of EugDotNet.

EugDotNet is an independent technology publication, engineering laboratory, professional directory, and community resource rooted in Eugene, Oregon, and the Pacific Northwest.

Its editorial purpose is to investigate, explain, document, and critically examine technology and its relationship to human life.

EugDotNet seeks to earn credibility through the quality of its work rather than through institutional size, commercial influence, or claims of authority.

These guidelines apply to human contributors and AI-assisted editorial workflows.

## 2. Editorial Mission

EugDotNet exists to:

- Make technical knowledge accessible.

- Document meaningful software engineering practices.

- Explore emerging technologies through practical experimentation.

- Examine the philosophical, cultural, economic, and social dimensions of technology.

- Connect regional technology professionals.

- Preserve the history of local technical communities.

- Encourage independent inquiry and intellectual curiosity.

- Provide useful resources for developers, learners, businesses, and community members.

The publication is regionally rooted but intellectually unrestricted.

Its coverage may extend beyond the Pacific Northwest whenever the subject is relevant to its editorial mission.

## 3. Foundational Principles

### 3.1 Accuracy

Seek accurate information and represent it faithfully.

Verify significant factual claims before publication.

Distinguish between:

- Established facts.

- Reported claims.

- Preliminary findings.

- Reasonable interpretations.

- Speculation.

- Personal opinions.

- Predictions.

Do not present uncertainty as certainty.

Do not confuse confidence of expression with strength of evidence.

### 3.2 Intellectual honesty

Represent evidence and arguments fairly, including those that challenge an author's preferred conclusions.

Avoid:

- Selective presentation of evidence.

- Misleading quotations.

- Straw-man arguments.

- Unsupported generalizations.

- Manufactured controversies.

- Exaggerated claims.

- Deliberate omission of relevant context.

Authors should be willing to revise their conclusions when better evidence becomes available.

### 3.3 Independence

Editorial decisions should be guided by relevance, evidence, public usefulness, and intellectual merit.

Coverage must not be dictated by:

- Advertisers.

- Sponsors.

- Vendors.

- Employers.

- Political organizations.

- Personal relationships.

- Commercial partnerships.

Relevant conflicts of interest must be disclosed.

### 3.4 Accountability

EugDotNet should accept responsibility for its published material.

Substantive errors should be corrected transparently.

Readers and subjects of coverage should have a reasonable means of requesting corrections or providing additional information.

### 3.5 Respect for persons

Treat people as individuals rather than merely as representatives of institutions, ideologies, demographic groups, or professional categories.

Criticism of ideas, conduct, institutions, and technologies is legitimate.

Personal attacks, harassment, gratuitous humiliation, and unnecessary invasions of privacy are not editorial objectives.

## 4. Intellectual Orientation

EugDotNet encourages interdisciplinary inquiry.

Technology may be examined through perspectives including:

- Software engineering.

- Computer science.

- Philosophy.

- History.

- Economics.

- Sociology.

- Political theory.

- Design.

- Linguistics.

- Education.

- Environmental studies.

- Indigenous knowledge and community perspectives.

No single discipline should be treated as sufficient to explain every technological phenomenon.

Interdisciplinary writing should nevertheless respect the methodological standards and limitations of the disciplines it engages.

Authors should distinguish philosophical argument from empirical research and technical demonstration.

The publication welcomes disagreement grounded in evidence, reasoning, and intellectual seriousness.

## 6. Editorial Classifications

Published material should be clearly classified according to its purpose.

| Classification | Purpose                                                    |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Reporting      | Document factual developments and events                   |
| Analysis       | Interpret evidence and explain significance                |
| Opinion        | Present an explicitly attributed argument                  |
| Essay          | Explore intellectual, cultural, or philosophical questions |
| Tutorial       | Teach a technical process or concept                       |
| Interview      | Present a conversation with a participant                  |
| Review         | Evaluate a technology, product, or resource                |
| Case Study     | Document a specific project or experience                  |
| Editorial      | Express an institutional editorial position                |
| Announcement   | Communicate publication or community information           |

These classifications should be visible where appropriate.

Opinion and analysis may contain factual claims that require verification.

Reporting should not conceal editorial advocacy behind ostensibly neutral language.

## 7. Reporting Standards

Reporting should establish:

- What happened.

- Who was involved.

- When it occurred.

- Where it occurred.

- What evidence supports the account.

- Why the development may be significant.

- What remains unknown.

Use primary sources whenever practical.

Primary sources may include:

- Interviews.

- Official documentation.

- Original research.

- Public records.

- Source code.

- Technical specifications.

- Direct observation.

- Original announcements.

- Recorded presentations.

Secondary sources may provide valuable context but should not automatically substitute for accessible primary evidence.

When reporting contested or consequential claims, seek relevant responses from affected parties where practical.

Do not imply that a party declined to comment unless a reasonable opportunity to respond was actually provided.

## 8. Source Evaluation

Sources should be evaluated according to their relevance and evidentiary quality.

Consider:

- Author expertise.

- Institutional context.

- Publication date.

- Research methodology.

- Availability of supporting evidence.

- Potential conflicts of interest.

- Independent corroboration.

- Whether the source is primary or secondary.

- Whether the source has been corrected or superseded.

Institutional prestige alone does not establish the truth of a claim.

Likewise, a source should not be dismissed solely because it lacks institutional prestige.

Technical documentation, academic literature, practitioner experience, and community knowledge may each provide valuable evidence within their appropriate contexts.

### 8.1 Social media

Social media may be used to identify developments, public statements, and community reactions.

Social media posts should not automatically be treated as reliable evidence of underlying factual claims.

Distinguish between:

- Evidence that a statement was made.

- Evidence that the statement is accurate.

### 8.2 Press releases

Press releases represent statements from interested parties.

Attribute claims appropriately.

Do not reproduce promotional language as independent reporting without evaluation.

### 8.3 Academic research

When discussing academic research, distinguish between:

- Peer-reviewed publications.

- Preprints.

- Conference presentations.

- Working papers.

- Institutional announcements.

- Independent interpretations of research.

Avoid treating a single study as definitive evidence of a broad conclusion.

## 9. Citations and References

EugDotNet should support both journalistic attribution and more formal academic referencing.

### 9.1 Inline attribution

Ordinary reporting should use readable inline attribution and hyperlinks.

Readers should be able to identify the origin of significant factual claims without unnecessary interruption.

### 9.2 Reference sections

Long-form essays, technical articles, research-oriented writing, and substantial analyses may include a references section.

References should include, where available:

- Author or organization.

- Title.

- Publication or publisher.

- Publication date.

- URL.

- DOI or persistent identifier.

- Access date when relevant.

### 9.3 Technical references

Technical articles should preferentially reference:

- Official documentation.

- Language specifications.

- Standards documents.

- Source repositories.

- Release notes.

- Relevant technical papers.

- Reproducible demonstrations.

Identify relevant software versions when behavior may vary between releases.

### 9.4 Philosophical and historical references

Philosophical and historical writing should identify the works being discussed.

Distinguish direct quotations from paraphrases and interpretations.

Include edition, chapter, section, or page information when relevant and available.

Avoid attributing popular paraphrases to historical thinkers as verified quotations.

### 9.5 Citation integrity

Never fabricate:

- Authors.

- Publications.

- Quotations.

- Research findings.

- URLs.

- DOI identifiers.

- Page numbers.

- Publication dates.

Every cited source should be checked before publication.

## 10. Quotations

Direct quotations must accurately represent the source.

Do not alter quotations in ways that change their meaning.

Use ellipses and editorial insertions responsibly.

Distinguish between:

- Verbatim quotation.

- Paraphrase.

- Summary.

- Reconstructed recollection.

- Translation.

Do not invent dialogue for documentary reporting.

Respect copyright and applicable quotation limitations.

Interview subjects should not be quoted as expressing views they did not express.

## 11. Technical Writing Standards

Technical writing should be useful, accurate, and sufficiently specific.

Where relevant, identify:

- Programming language.

- Framework version.

- Runtime version.

- Operating system.

- Relevant dependencies.

- Configuration requirements.

- Hardware assumptions.

- Environmental limitations.

### 11.1 Code examples

Code examples should be:

- Readable.

- Appropriately scoped.

- Secure by default where practical.

- Tested when represented as working examples.

- Consistent with the accompanying explanation.

Distinguish between:

- Tested implementation.

- Illustrative example.

- Pseudocode.

- Incomplete prototype.

- Conceptual architecture.

Do not present generated or untested code as verified working software.

### 11.2 Tutorials

Tutorials should identify:

- Intended audience.

- Prerequisites.

- Required tools.

- Expected outcome.

- Important implementation steps.

- Common limitations or failure conditions.

Where commands could cause destructive changes, clearly explain the consequences.

Do not expose actual credentials, private infrastructure details, or sensitive client information.

### 11.3 Reproducibility

Technical claims should be reproducible where practical.

Laboratory articles may include:

- Repository links.

- Version identifiers.

- Configuration examples.

- Test cases.

- Experimental conditions.

- Measurements.

- Known limitations.

A failed experiment may be worth publishing when its methodology and findings provide useful knowledge.

## 12. Laboratory Standards

The EugDotNet Lab is a space for investigation rather than an exclusively promotional showcase.

Laboratory projects should communicate:

- The problem or question being investigated.

- The intended objective.

- The technical approach.

- Relevant implementation decisions.

- Observations and results.

- Known limitations.

- Current development status.

- Potential future directions.

Distinguish observations from interpretations.

Do not exaggerate the maturity, reliability, or performance of experimental software.

Benchmarks should describe their methodology and relevant environmental conditions.

Negative results and abandoned approaches may be documented when they contribute useful knowledge.

## 13. Artificial Intelligence Coverage

AI-related reporting and analysis should maintain the same evidentiary standards applied to other technologies.

Distinguish between:

- Demonstrated capabilities.

- Vendor claims.

- Research findings.

- Benchmarks.

- Anecdotal experiences.

- Forecasts.

- Speculative scenarios.

- Marketing terminology.

Avoid treating predictions as inevitable outcomes.

Avoid anthropomorphic descriptions when they obscure the actual operation or limitations of a system.

Do not infer general capability from a narrowly demonstrated task without appropriate qualification.

Discuss technological benefits, limitations, risks, and trade-offs according to the available evidence.

## 14. AI-Assisted Editorial Work

EugDotNet recognizes AI systems as potentially useful editorial and technical tools.

Permitted uses may include:

- Brainstorming.

- Research assistance.

- Structural editing.

- Copyediting.

- Grammar and spelling assistance.

- Technical explanation.

- Code generation.

- Code review.

- Translation assistance.

- Summarization.

- Diagram development.

- Illustrative image generation.

AI assistance does not replace editorial responsibility.

### 14.1 Human accountability

Every published article must have an accountable human author or editor.

The responsible human must review and approve:

- Factual claims.

- Quotations.

- References.

- Technical examples.

- Interpretations.

- Editorial conclusions.

- Publication readiness.

An AI system must not be represented as having conducted interviews, attended events, personally tested software, or directly observed circumstances it did not experience.

### 14.2 Verification

AI-generated factual claims must be independently checked.

AI-generated references must be verified against actual sources.

AI-generated code must be tested before being described as working.

AI-generated summaries must be compared with the relevant original material.

### 14.3 Disclosure

Material AI involvement should be disclosed when it is relevant to a reader's understanding of how an article, experiment, or illustration was produced.

Routine spelling correction or minor copyediting does not necessarily require individual disclosure.

Substantial AI-generated prose, experimental AI outputs, and AI-generated illustrations may warrant explicit disclosure.

AI-generated images must not be presented as documentary photographs.

### 14.4 Confidentiality

Do not submit the following to external AI systems without appropriate authorization:

- Confidential interviews.

- Unpublished sensitive material.

- Private correspondence.

- Client source code.

- Credentials.

- Personal information.

- Proprietary documentation.

- Confidential source identities.

AI tools should receive only the information necessary for their approved task.

### 14.5 Publication authority

AI systems must not independently publish articles, approve editorial changes, issue corrections, contact sources, or represent EugDotNet externally without explicit authorization.

## 15. Interviews

Interviews should seek to understand the participant's experience, knowledge, and perspective.

Interview subjects should ordinarily understand:

- The identity of the publication.

- The general purpose of the interview.

- Whether their statements are intended for publication.

- Whether the conversation is being recorded.

- Any agreed attribution conditions.

Obtain appropriate consent before recording.

Respect applicable recording laws.

Clarify attribution conditions before accepting sensitive information.

### 15.1 Interview editing

Interviews may be edited for length, clarity, and readability.

Editing must not materially distort the participant's meaning.

If an interview is substantially edited or condensed, indicate this where appropriate.

### 15.2 Subject review

Interview subjects may be invited to verify technical details or quotations.

Such review should not automatically confer editorial control over the entire article.

Editorial independence remains with EugDotNet.

## 16. Confidential Sources

Confidential sources should be used cautiously.

Before accepting confidential information, consider:

- Public significance.

- Source credibility.

- Potential harm.

- Verification possibilities.

- Legal implications.

- The publication's ability to protect confidentiality.

Do not promise confidentiality that the publication cannot reasonably maintain.

Confidential source information must not be stored in public repositories, ordinary publication metadata, or unauthorized external services.

The use of anonymous or confidential sources should be explained to readers when doing so does not compromise the source.

## 17. Opinion and Philosophical Writing

EugDotNet welcomes reasoned opinion, philosophical inquiry, and intellectual disagreement.

Authors may defend substantive positions.

They should nevertheless:

- Identify important assumptions.

- Define relevant concepts.

- Distinguish empirical claims from normative claims.

- Engage meaningful counterarguments.

- Represent opposing positions fairly.

- Acknowledge uncertainty.

- Avoid substituting rhetoric for evidence.

Editorial independence does not require the absence of judgment.

It requires that judgments remain accountable to evidence, reasoning, and transparent argument.

The publication should encourage inquiry rather than ideological conformity.

## 18. Technology Reviews

Reviews should disclose relevant evaluation conditions.

Where applicable, identify:

- Product or software version.

- Evaluation period.

- Relevant hardware.

- Testing methodology.

- Whether access was purchased, provided, or sponsored.

- Relevant commercial relationships.

- Known evaluation limitations.

Do not present vendor-provided demonstrations as independent testing.

Do not guarantee favorable coverage in exchange for access, compensation, or equipment.

A review may reach a strongly favorable or unfavorable conclusion when supported by the evidence.

## 19. Conflicts of Interest

Contributors should disclose relationships that could reasonably affect a reader's interpretation of published material.

Potential conflicts include:

- Employment.

- Consulting relationships.

- Financial interests.

- Sponsorship.

- Affiliate compensation.

- Personal relationships.

- Ownership of reviewed products.

- Participation in covered projects.

- Membership in covered organizations.

EugDotNet's founder may operate independent software development and consulting businesses.

Coverage involving those businesses should clearly disclose the relationship.

Disclosure does not automatically prohibit publication.

However, some conflicts may require independent review, reassignment, or a decision not to publish.

## 20. Sponsorship and Advertising

EugDotNet may consider sponsorship or advertising in the future.

Any such arrangement must preserve editorial independence.

Sponsored material must be clearly identified.

Paid placement must not be represented as independent reporting.

Sponsors must not receive undisclosed editorial control.

Directory participation and editorial coverage must not be conditioned on purchasing advertising.

Affiliate links, when used, must be disclosed appropriately.

Commercial arrangements should not compromise the publication's credibility or community responsibilities.

## 21. Developer Directory Integrity

The developer directory is a professional discovery resource rather than an editorial ranking system.

Participation must be voluntary.

Directory records should contain only approved public professional information.

Do not:

- Fabricate profiles.

- Scrape personal information to create unsolicited profiles.

- Invent qualifications.

- Assign unsupported ratings.

- Guarantee professional competence.

- Misrepresent availability.

- Imply endorsements that have not been made.

Availability should be identified as self-reported and dated.

Directory inclusion does not constitute a guarantee of a developer's suitability for a particular engagement.

Editorial coverage and directory participation are separate activities.

## 22. Regional and Community Coverage

EugDotNet should document the regional technology community with care and accuracy.

Community coverage should:

- Attribute work to its actual contributors.

- Preserve meaningful historical records.

- Respect volunteer labor.

- Recognize independent and smaller-scale initiatives.

- Avoid exaggerating attendance or organizational influence.

- Distinguish planned activities from completed activities.

- Identify external event organizers accurately.

The publication should remain open to regional developers and technical professionals regardless of their preferred programming language or technology ecosystem.

Its .NET origins should be preserved without becoming an unnecessary barrier to participation.

## 23. Indigenous and Community Knowledge

When covering Indigenous technology initiatives, language technologies, cultural projects, or community knowledge, EugDotNet should respect the authority and protocols of the communities involved.

Public accessibility does not automatically establish permission to reproduce or repurpose cultural knowledge.

Consider:

- Community consent.

- Cultural protocols.

- Appropriate attribution.

- Indigenous data sovereignty.

- Collective as well as individual interests.

- Restrictions on sensitive knowledge.

- The distinction between public documentation and community-governed information.

Do not assume that all knowledge should be made openly accessible.

Seek appropriate guidance when the publication lacks the relevant cultural or institutional knowledge.

## 24. Historical Preservation

The original EugDotNet meetup archive represents genuine community activity.

Historical records should preserve:

- Original dates.

- Presentation titles.

- Speaker attribution.

- Organizer attribution.

- Available photographs.

- Presentation materials.

- Relevant announcements.

- Existing contextual information.

Historical records should not be rewritten to exaggerate the success, scale, or continuity of the organization.

Later editorial annotations should be distinguishable from original records.

Where information is incomplete, acknowledge the limitation rather than reconstructing unsupported details.

## 25. Privacy and Harm

The publication should balance public usefulness against foreseeable harm.

Avoid publishing unnecessary:

- Private addresses.

- Personal contact information.

- Sensitive personal records.

- Confidential correspondence.

- Private credentials.

- Security-sensitive infrastructure details.

- Information that creates an unreasonable risk of harassment.

Public availability of information does not automatically establish editorial justification for republication.

Consider whether a less intrusive means can accomplish the same legitimate editorial purpose.

## 26. Security Reporting

Technical security coverage should be accurate and responsible.

When discussing vulnerabilities:

- Identify affected software and versions where appropriate.

- Verify claims.

- Distinguish confirmed vulnerabilities from suspected weaknesses.

- Describe available mitigations.

- Respect responsible disclosure practices.

- Avoid unnecessarily exposing private systems or credentials.

Do not conduct unauthorized security testing on third-party systems.

Laboratory security demonstrations should use authorized, isolated, or intentionally vulnerable environments.

## 27. Images and Visual Evidence

Images should be classified according to their purpose.

Potential classifications include:

- Documentary photograph.

- Software screenshot.

- Technical diagram.

- Original illustration.

- Archival image.

- Stock image.

- AI-generated illustration.

Do not present illustrative imagery as evidence of an actual event.

Documentary photographs should not be materially manipulated in ways that misrepresent the circumstances depicted.

Ordinary cropping, exposure correction, and similar adjustments may be acceptable when they do not alter documentary meaning.

Image captions should identify relevant context and attribution.

Respect licensing and applicable consent requirements.

## 28. Editorial Voice and Style

EugDotNet should maintain a voice that is:

- Intellectually engaged.

- Technically competent.

- Accessible.

- Curious.

- Precise.

- Independent.

- Occasionally conversational.

- Appropriate to the subject matter.

Avoid unnecessary corporate language, promotional exaggeration, and formulaic writing.

Do not assume readers are experts in every discipline.

Explain specialized terminology when appropriate without oversimplifying technical material.

Authors may retain distinctive individual voices.

The publication should not impose a uniform personality on every contributor.

Humor, wit, and criticism are legitimate when they serve the material.

## 29. Article Structure

A substantial article may include:

- Headline.

- Subtitle.

- Summary.

- Author attribution.

- Publication date.

- Editorial classification.

- Introduction.

- Main argument or narrative.

- Supporting evidence.

- Technical examples.

- Relevant limitations.

- Conclusion.

- References.

- Disclosures.

- Related material.

Not every article requires every element.

Structure should serve the content rather than impose unnecessary formalism.

Headlines should accurately represent the article.

Avoid misleading headlines designed primarily to attract clicks.

## 30. Corrections and Updates

Substantive factual errors should be corrected promptly.

A correction should ordinarily identify:

- What was incorrect.

- What has been corrected.

- The date of the correction.

- Relevant explanatory context.

Preserve the original publication date.

Distinguish corrections from:

- Typographical changes.

- Formatting improvements.

- Additional reporting.

- Updated technical instructions.

- Changes in an author's interpretation.

Do not silently rewrite substantial factual claims in ways that obscure the publication's previous errors.

Significant retractions should be identified transparently.

## 31. Reader Feedback and Right of Reply

Readers should have a reasonable means of contacting the publication.

Feedback may include:

- Factual corrections.

- Technical corrections.

- Additional sources.

- Alternative interpretations.

- Article proposals.

- Community announcements.

- Concerns about coverage.

The publication is not obligated to publish every response.

Substantive responses that materially improve the public record should receive appropriate consideration.

Disagreement alone is not a sufficient reason to dismiss relevant evidence.

## 32. Contributor Participation

EugDotNet may accept contributions from developers, researchers, educators, writers, and community members.

Potential contributions include:

- Articles.

- Essays.

- Tutorials.

- Interviews.

- Technical demonstrations.

- Photography.

- Illustration.

- Event documentation.

- Reviews.

- Research assistance.

Contributors should understand the publication's editorial standards before submitting material.

Contributions must be original or appropriately licensed.

Plagiarism is unacceptable.

Editorial acceptance does not automatically transfer copyright ownership.

Any publication rights, licensing terms, or compensation arrangements should be established explicitly.

Do not assume contributors have agreed to unpaid work, copyright transfer, or unrestricted reuse.

## 33. Editorial Review Process

The initial editorial workflow may be maintained through repository-based content and ordinary human review.

A proposed workflow is:

1. Identify a subject or editorial question.

2. Determine the article classification.

3. Gather relevant sources.

4. Conduct reporting, research, or experimentation.

5. Prepare a draft.

6. Review factual claims.

7. Verify quotations and references.

8. Review technical examples.

9. Review conflicts of interest and disclosures.

10. Edit for clarity and structure.

11. Review accessibility and media attribution.

12. Approve publication.

13. Publish through the authorized process.

14. Address subsequent corrections or updates.

The depth of review should reflect the significance and complexity of the material.

A short community announcement does not require the same review process as a substantial investigative or technical article.

## 34. Editorial Independence and Institutional Identity

EugDotNet is not automatically a representative of:

- The City of Eugene.

- The State of Oregon.

- Microsoft.

- The .NET Foundation.

- Local universities.

- Regional businesses.

- Technology vendors.

- Political organizations.

- Other community institutions.

Affiliations and partnerships should be described accurately.

The publication should maintain a distinction between its institutional editorial position and the personal opinions of individual contributors.

The founder and editor may express personal views without implying that every contributor or directory participant shares those views.

## 35. Sustainable Publishing

EugDotNet should prioritize quality and continuity over publication volume.

Do not establish an editorial schedule that cannot reasonably be maintained.

The publication may grow through:

- Occasional substantial articles.

- Practical technical notes.

- Interviews.

- Laboratory findings.

- Community documentation.

- Guest contributions.

- Incremental improvements to existing material.

Existing articles may be updated when technical developments warrant revision.

Do not publish low-quality material merely to satisfy an arbitrary content quota.

A modest but reliable publication is preferable to an ambitious publication that rapidly becomes inactive.

## 36. Editorial Standards for AI Coding Agents

AI coding agents implementing EugDotNet must respect these editorial requirements.

The application should support:

- Accurate author attribution.

- Editorial classifications.

- Publication dates.

- References.

- Corrections.

- Disclosures.

- Media attribution.

- Publication lifecycle states.

- Historical records.

- Contributor identity.

- Directory consent requirements.

AI coding agents must not:

- Invent production articles.

- Fabricate interviews.

- Generate fictional testimonials for public use.

- Invent professional directory participants.

- Fabricate historical meetup records.

- Publish unverified claims.

- Automatically approve editorial material.

- Represent placeholder content as authentic reporting.

Development fixtures must remain clearly identified and excluded from production publication.

Editorial functionality should be implemented incrementally according to the approved content model and implementation plan.

## 38. Revision of Editorial Guidelines

These guidelines should evolve as EugDotNet develops.

Substantive changes should be documented.

Potential reasons for revision include:

- New contributors.

- Expanded editorial coverage.

- New publication formats.

- Commercial relationships.

- Emerging technologies.

- Changes in legal or professional standards.

- Lessons learned from editorial practice.

Editorial policies should remain practical and proportionate to the publication's size.

## 39. Definition of Editorial Success

EugDotNet should be considered editorially successful when:

- Readers can trust its factual claims.

- Technical material provides genuine practical value.

- Authors receive accurate attribution.

- Intellectual disagreement is handled seriously.

- Sources are represented fairly.

- Errors are corrected transparently.

- Community relationships are treated responsibly.

- Commercial interests remain distinguishable from editorial judgment.

- Historical material is preserved.

- The publication contributes useful knowledge to its readers.

The objective is not to claim authority over technology or its interpretation.

The objective is to become a trustworthy participant in the ongoing investigation, development, and public understanding of technology.
